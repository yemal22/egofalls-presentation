Question / Why this matters

A fall can become an emergency.

842,000

Estimated deaths from falls worldwide in 2023.

WHO Global Health Estimates · published October 2026

Why detect it?

A person who falls may need assistance. Automatic detection could support an alert when nobody witnesses the event.

Our intended use: a wearable detector that could notify a caregiver.

Source: WHO · Falls (2026). Alert delivery and clinical benefit remain outside this experiment.

Question / What the camera sees

The camera moves with the person.

Real recordings of staged activities · four fall types and eight non-fall activities.

Image: Wang · EGOFALLS dataset repository. Staged recordings do not establish performance on accidental falls.

Question / The technical challenge

Movement alone can be misleading.

Coverage

A wearable camera follows the person beyond one fixed camera's field of view.

Ambiguity

Bending, sitting or handling the camera can also create sudden motion.

Complementarity

Impact or vocal sounds may help—but everyday sounds can also mislead.

Context: Wang · EGOFALLS (2023). Wearable video and audio still raise privacy and practical-use questions.

Does listening improve fall detection?

Can combining egocentric video and audio detect more falls, while keeping false alarms low, on people unseen during training?

03 / Before feature extraction

Inspect the signal before trusting it.

Quality

Decode failures, brightness, blur and clip duration.

Motion

Frame differences, optical flow and camera movement.

Coverage

Compare classes and participants; review representative clips.

Large motion is an observation—not proof of a fall. Confirm interpretations against the video.
